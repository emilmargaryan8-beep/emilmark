The Other’s Shadow

Emil Margaryan

🇫🇷 Français🇬🇧 English

Short story · 2026 · French original

At ten o’clock, the light still entered Seville’s streets at an angle.

It slid across the whitewashed façades, woke the blue of the azulejos and traced wrought-iron balconies onto the paving stones. It reached faces without flattening them and stretched shadows sharp enough to suggest a second gesture. It was Sébastien Gennetier’s favourite hour.

He was looking for neither the Giralda nor the carriages lined up beside the cathedral. Postcards already knew how to show them.

He preferred streets where laundry dried behind iron grilles, waiters stacked chairs, and residents crossed squares without looking up at what visitors admired.

People who were waiting interested him more. Their bodies often said what their faces tried to conceal: a hand returning to an empty pocket, a foot turned towards departure, a chin lifting at every sound of footsteps.

Sébastien knew his method resembled theft.

That Tuesday, near the Plaza del Salvador, a young woman was waiting for someone.
