The Man Who Attended Other People’s Weddings

Emil Margaryan

🇫🇷 Français🇬🇧 English🇷🇺 Русский

Short story · 2026 · French original

Artur Figueira had attended one hundred and forty-seven weddings and had been invited to only three.

Those three invitations dated back to a time when his hair was black, his suits were too large, and newlyweds were still young enough to believe that a guest list could predict the future.

For the other one hundred and forty-four, Artur had come on his own.

He preferred Saturday weddings. On Sundays, people paid more attention.

On Saturdays, everyone had something to do: straighten a tie, seat a grandmother, stop a child from licking the flowers, dislike the photographer. A well-dressed fifty-eight-year-old man could enter a church among a hundred people without belonging to anyone.

Artur had learned to arrive after the relatives and before the latecomers.

That Saturday in June, the wedding was taking place in a small church in Graça. Artur arrived at 4:12 for a ceremony announced for four o’clock.

Perfect.

The bride was not there.
