The Reverse Side of Escape

Emil Margaryan

🇫🇷 Français🇬🇧 English

Short story · 2026 · French original

Elena left rehearsal with the feeling that she had spent three hours learning how not to be seen.

She had been given the role of an attendant. Again. Two entrances in the first act, a crossing behind the princess, six measures downstage during which she had to turn her eyes away so that the audience would see only the soloist.

“Lighter, Elena,” Anders had said, clapping twice. “You dance as though your place depended on it.”

She had almost replied that this was precisely the problem.

At thirty-two, she knew the theatre’s corridors better than some of its stages.

That evening Stockholm was damp and almost colourless. Instead of taking the bus, she headed towards Skeppsholmen.

On the bridge, a man was watching her.

Not the way one watches a passer-by. As though he could already see a painting inside her.

Elena slowed despite herself.
