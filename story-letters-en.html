Letters from No One

Emil Margaryan

🇫🇷 Français🇬🇧 English🇷🇺 Русский

English literary version of the French original

The first letter arrived on a Tuesday, between a telephone bill and an advertisement for furniture Gabriel Vernet would never have bought.

The envelope was white. His name in black ink. No return address.

He opened it in the hall.

“There's a night you've forgotten. I'm going to give it back to you.”

Gabriel turned the sheet over. Nothing. The postmark was Geneva, the day before.

His first instinct was the same as when he found a glaring error in a manuscript: look for what did not fit. The sentence was too sure of itself. It presumed an intimacy no one had with him.

At forty-one, Gabriel worked as a proofreader for a small publishing house near Plainpalais. He spent his days removing unnecessary words, spotting contradictions, asking authors how a character could be in Paris in chapter four if he did not leave Lyon until chapter five.

He liked things that could be repaired in pencil.

The letter offered nothing to correct.

He slipped it into a book and went to work.
