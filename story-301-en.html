The Three Hundred and First Resident

Emil Margaryan

🇫🇷 Français🇬🇧 English🇷🇺 Русский

English literary translation of the French original

The bus dropped Arpi Vardanyan beside a sign from which the village’s name had lost three letters.

All that remained was DZOR—HEN. The wind took care of the rest.

—The town hall? she asked the driver.

—The house with the flag.

He thought about it.

—Or the one with the dog. The flag changes houses when it rains.

Arpi got off with her suitcase, her tablet, and her orders from the Statistics Committee. She was thirty-one and believed in numbers because they alone did not change their story when questioned separately.

In five years of service, she had come across dead people who still voted, living people born under two spellings, and a shepherd registered as unmarried in three municipalities. She had put them back in their boxes without anger. Once corrected, an error seemed almost grateful.

And yet, folded at the bottom of her satchel, she kept an old woman’s letter of complaint. On her first inspection, Arpi had removed a duplicate entry and, with it, the last administrative reason for a nurse to climb to the woman’s hamlet every week. The number had become accurate. The old woman had been forced to walk down.

Dzorashen was the first village where an error seemed to have been waiting for her.
